# The primer and its head name a guard's issue under two conditions

## Summary

`primer@where-knowledge-goes` says a guard, a workaround, a stub or a test "that exists because of
an issue names it in the comment at the site". The head it restates,
`design@agent-skills@a-reference-claims-a-revisit`, names the issue in the comment of one "that pins
behaviour an open entry describes". A test that pins a behaviour an open entry describes, and was
not written because of that entry, owes the reference under the head and not under the primer.
Which condition is meant?

## Details

### What

The two texts:

- `path@agent-skills@content/PRIMER.md`, the reference sentence of its section `where-knowledge-goes`:
  "a guard, a workaround, a stub or a test that exists because of an issue names it in the comment
  at the site".
- `register@agent-skills@design`, the table of `design@agent-skills@a-reference-claims-a-revisit`:
  "a guard, a workaround, a stub or a test that pins behaviour an open entry describes | the issue it
  exists because of, in the comment at the site".

The head's row mixes both: its first cell uses "pins behaviour an open entry describes", its second
"the issue it exists because of". The root CLAUDE.md of this repository restated the head's row until
the work that made the primer and the root one text removed it, so the session that reads both now
meets only the primer's condition. Found by the fidelity-of-relocation review of that work.

### Why it matters

`goal@knowledge-architect@agents-get-a-complete-workflow` asks that each instruction be plain enough
to follow without guessing. A session writing a test that pins a behaviour an open issue describes
can read either condition, and under one of them it writes no reference, so closing the issue does
not reach the test, against the reason the head gives: "closing the entry dangles the comment, so
the site is revisited".

### What would close it

A ruling, under `skill@knowledge-architect-design` if the wider condition is a change of the
decision, on which condition the head means; then the primer's sentence and the head's row brought
to it in one change.
