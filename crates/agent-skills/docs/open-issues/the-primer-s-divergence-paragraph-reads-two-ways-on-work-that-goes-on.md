# The primer's paragraph on a divergence between a head and the code reads two ways on the work that goes on meanwhile

## Summary

The first bullet of `primer@intent-and-claims` says, where a session finds a head's argument
defeated, that "the part of the task that rests on the point in divergence waits for that answer,
and the rest of the task goes on". It ends: "Work on that point that goes on meanwhile builds on the
head." Does work on the point in divergence wait, or go on building on the head?

## Details

### What

Two census agents of the design discussion on what enters agent-facing text reported the two
sentences as possibly contradictory. Two readings:

- the last sentence names work on the point that does not rest on the reversal, such as a fix
  that the head and the reversal would both keep, which goes on on the head;
- the last sentence names the work the first sentence holds, which then both waits and goes on.

`design@agent-skills@head-prevails-over-diverging-code` says "work that goes on meanwhile builds on
the head", and its argument is that a session "that must keep working would build on whichever side
it met first". Not established: which work on the point the primer means to go on.

### Why it matters

`goal@knowledge-architect@agents-get-a-complete-workflow` asks that each instruction say what it
asks "plainly enough to follow without guessing what it means". A session meeting a divergence
either holds work it could have done or builds on a head that the owner may reverse.

### What would close it

A reading of `design@agent-skills@head-prevails-over-diverging-code` and
`design@agent-skills@divergence-routed-by-its-authority` that states which work waits; then a
rewording of the primer's bullet to that reading, or, where neither head states it, the owner's
ruling on it.
