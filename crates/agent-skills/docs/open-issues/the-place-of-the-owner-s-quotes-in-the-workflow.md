# What place, and how much weight, verbatim quotations of the owner have in the workflow

## Summary

The workflow quotes the owner's words verbatim in many records: a design head whose ground is the
owner's, test 4 of the entry tests, plan documents, commit messages, retrospective analyses, and
the audit checks that read a quotation against the question it answered. The owner holds these
quotations of little value most of the time, as long as the attribution is correct, and asks that
their place and importance in the workflow be rethought. The owner's words are hedged positions to
argue, so the question is argued in a design discussion and not applied.

## Details

### What

The owner's words, in the order given:

- Ruling on pass 1 of this repository's design-record audit: "Agreed on all defaults except F2 and
  F4: no need to quote my words, as long as attribution is correct." F2 asked to quote the owner's
  scope ruling in `design@knowledge-architect@versioning-policy`. F4 asked to quote the owner's
  request in `design@core@milestone-entries-are-directories`. Each now attributes the decision to
  the owner without quoting.
- On the rule itself, at the same ruling: "I'm tempted to remove this rule too. It's not
  particularly relevant IMO. It is weird to keep those verbatim quotes in design heads anyway."
- Ruling on the intake of the retrospectives of 2026-10-08 to 2026-10-10, on a finding that no
  checked form quotes text holding the checker's syntax: "IMO, the problem here is the other way
  around: I don't consider these owner quotes very valuable most of the  time, as long as
  attribution is correct. I do not want to provide this feature from the checker. The design
  question to ba recorded in the issue is instead, rethink the place and importance of owner quotes
  in the workflow."

The rule and its delivery, as they stand:

- `primer@design-heads`: "The owner's words are a ground, quoted, only where the decision came from
  the owner"; and test 4 of the entry tests, "The head quotes the owner's words and the owner's
  answer".
- The design-record auditor's failure mode about an owner quotation answering another question, and
  the verifiers' check of a quotation of the owner against the question it answered.
- Many heads of this repository quote the owner, and plan documents, commit messages and
  retrospective analyses quote rulings verbatim.

The evidence the intake added: two sessions bent a verbatim quotation to get it past the checker.
In the agent-configuration-entities milestone, the owner's words held spans in the grammar of new
reference kinds, which would have dangled once the kinds existed, and were written as angle-bracket
placeholders, so the quotation was no longer verbatim. In the session that wrote
`spec@plans@path-quickfixes`, the owner's words naming the reference form of a skill and an agent
were written bare, beside no issue entry, which `design@agent-skills@plain-text-is-no-repair` does
not admit. The cost of keeping a quotation verbatim then falls on the checker's syntax.

The suspected mechanism, in one sentence: the workflow treats the owner's exact words as the
evidence of what the owner decided, where a correct attribution serves the same purpose in most
records.

Re-entry point: the design discussion that takes up this entry.

### Why it matters

The quotation rule shapes every head whose ground is the owner's, every audit pass that reads heads,
and every record of a ruling. Its decision is `design@agent-skills@head-ground-is-the-argument`,
which this question would change. `goal@knowledge-architect@the-owner-decides` is what a quotation
serves: a later session can tell what the owner said from what an agent inferred. The question is
whether attribution serves it as well, and where a verbatim quotation is still owed.

### What would close it

A design discussion under `skill@knowledge-architect-design` that sets where the workflow quotes
the owner verbatim, where it attributes, and where neither is owed, across heads, plan documents,
commit messages and the records of reviews and audits. If the rule changes, the audit standards
that check quotations change with it, and a Migration entry names the design-record axis.
